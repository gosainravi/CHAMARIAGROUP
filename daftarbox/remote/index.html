      Stay safe from remote-access scams

      Scammers love remote-access apps. Two minutes here can save your savings.

      The golden rule

      If someone called, messaged or emailed you first, never give them your ID or password. Only share them with a person you contacted yourself and know well.

      • “Digital arrest”

        A video call from fake police, CBI, customs or a court. They say a parcel or your Aadhaar is linked to a crime and keep you “under watch” until you pay.

        There is no such thing as a digital arrest. Real police never do this on a call.

      • Bank or KYC update

        “Your KYC has expired, your account will be blocked today.” Then they ask you to install an app so they can “help”.

        Banks never ask you to install remote-access apps. Visit your branch.

      • Fake customer care

        You search for a helpline and call a number on a fake website. The “agent” wants to connect to your phone or PC to fix a problem.

        Only use numbers from the official app, card or bill.

      • Refund or cashback

        “We sent you too much money by mistake” or “Install this to receive your refund.” Then they move money out while connected.

        You never need an app or your UPI PIN to receive money.

      Warning signs

      • They rush you: “do it now or your account closes”.
      • They ask you to keep it secret from family.
      • They ask for OTP, UPI PIN, card number or passwords.
      • They want you to open your bank app while connected.
      • They ask you to turn off “Ask me before every connection”.

      If it already happened

      1. End the session and turn off Wi-Fi or mobile data.
      2. Call your bank using the number on your card and block cards and UPI.
      3. Call 1930, the national cyber crime helpline, as fast as you can – the first hours matter most.
      4. Report online at cybercrime.gov.in.
      5. Uninstall unknown apps and change your passwords from a different device.
