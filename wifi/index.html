      How it works

      Plain answers about what the tool does and doesn't do.

      It's a prediction, not a measurement

      A web page can't read real Wi-Fi signal. We predict it from the drawing: distance, walls in the way and each access point's power. Furniture, people, glass and devices change real results, so plan with a little headroom. A walk-through survey app is coming later.

      How we read your drawing

      • Walls. We look for the double lines architects use for brick and block walls, plus solid columns. Text, door swings and dimension lines are ignored.
      • Floor area. The space inside the outer walls, with door openings closed off. Title blocks and notes outside the building are left out.
      • Scale. We read the millimetre dimensions on the drawing (on scans we use text recognition) and match each to its dimension line. If a PDF says "1:100" we use that too. If nothing works we ask one question: roughly how big is the floor.
      • Access points. Placed where they cover the most floor, away from walls and spread out, until the target share of the floor gets the signal you picked on 5 GHz. 2.4 GHz is reported too. Channels are picked so neighbours don't clash.

      Anything wrong? Open Fix walls or AP positions on the results screen.
